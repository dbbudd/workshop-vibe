Apple · Getting started

Xcode

Xcode is Apple’s free app for making apps for iPhone, iPad and Mac. You build in Swift, and Xcode 27 has AI agents built in, such as Claude Agent and ChatGPT’s Codex, that plan, write and test the code with you.

Best for

A tool that should be a real app on a student’s iPad: it works offline, keeps data on the device, and needs no sign-in.

Start here

Xcode 27, free from the Mac App Store. It needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) on macOS 26.6 or later.
Swift Playground, on iPad or Mac, is a gentler start, but has no AI chat.

The AI agents come from Anthropic, OpenAI and Google, and neither Anthropic nor OpenAI lists Hong Kong as a supported region. Check with HKIS IT before you rely on them.

Exercise 1: Install Xcode and start a project

  1. Install Xcode from the Mac App Store, and open it. The first time, choose Download & Install to add the simulators and tools.
  2. In the Welcome to Xcode window, choose App from the New Project menu. Xcode makes a SwiftUI app called MyApp straight away.
  3. Choose File, then Save Project, to name it and choose where it goes.

Save it in a new folder in your home folder, not in Documents, Desktop, Downloads or iCloud Drive: macOS can stop the AI agent from working there.

Exercise 2: Build one screen with AI

Set up an agent once. Then describe your screen with the prompt spine.

  1. Choose Xcode, then Settings…, then Intelligence.
  2. Under Agents, click Get beside Claude Agent, then Install, and sign in with your Claude account. Or, under Chat, turn on ChatGPT in Xcode, which works without an account.
  3. Open the coding assistant with ⌘0, click New Conversation, choose your agent, and send the prompt spine.
The prompt spine, for an app

Build a SwiftUI screen for a Grade 7 student who loses their place in a multi-step maths problem. Show the current step first, then let them tick it off. Keep the language at Grade 5.

Claude Agent needs a paid Claude plan (Pro or Max), and its use counts against that plan’s limits. ChatGPT in Xcode works without an account, with usage limits.

Exercise 3: Run it

  1. Choose an iPad from the run destination menu in the toolbar, then click Run (⌘R). Your app opens in an iPad simulator, in Device Hub.
  2. To run it on your own iPad: sign in under Xcode, Settings…, Apple Accounts, and in Signing & Capabilities turn on Automatically manage signing. Connect the iPad, tap Trust, and on the iPad turn on Developer Mode, in Settings, then Privacy & Security. Then choose the iPad, and click Run.

With a free Apple Account, an app on your own iPad stops working after 7 days, and you run it again from Xcode. A free account can use up to 3 devices.

Exercise 4: Get it to students

TestFlight is for testing with colleagues: students’ school Apple Accounts can’t use it. To put the app on HKIS iPads, publish it as a private app for HKIS through Apple School Manager, which HKIS then installs, or put it on the App Store. The App Store guide walks you through both.
